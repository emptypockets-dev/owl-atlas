01 / Preferred Pi-style candidate

Pi III · Athena’s helmet, in detail

BnF 1966.453.1475 · 17.19 g · 21 mm

Bibliothèque nationale de France, département Monnaies, médailles et antiques, 1966.453.1475; Gallica, via Wikimedia Commons.

Identification

Pi III, as catalogued by BnF. SNG France 1, Collection Jean et Marie Delepierre, no. 1475.

BnF specimen catalogue (opens in a new tab) · IRIS type record (opens in a new tab) · Gallica record (opens in a new tab)

Dating

c. 353–295 BCE (BnF catalogue, attributed there to Flament).

This is the specimen catalogue’s date range, not an exact issue date and not a claim that all Pi-style chronology is settled.

The full obverse preserves Athena’s profile eye and the helmet ornament. The reverse gives a separate, close view of the owl and surrounding details. Identification comes from the catalogue, not from appearance alone.

02 / Preferred later Old Style candidate

Quadridigité · A named later Old Style group

BnF 1966.453.1478 · 17.22 g · 21 mm

Bibliothèque nationale de France, département Monnaies, médailles et antiques, 1966.453.1478; Gallica, via Wikimedia Commons.

Identification

Quadridigité, as catalogued by BnF. SNG France 1, Collection Jean et Marie Delepierre, no. 1478.

BnF specimen catalogue (opens in a new tab) · IRIS type record (opens in a new tab) · Gallica record (opens in a new tab)

Dating

c. 286–261 BCE (BnF catalogue, attributed there to Flament).

The source’s date convention is preserved. Other specialist catalogues may draw different endpoints.

Both faces are available as full photographs. Compare the helmet treatment with the Pi III example, while retaining the named catalogue classification. This specimen illustrates one later Old Style group, not the whole third century.

03 / Additional Pi-style comparison

Pi II · An additional securely catalogued example

BnF 1966.453.1469 · 17.16 g · 23 mm

Bibliothèque nationale de France, département Monnaies, médailles et antiques, 1966.453.1469; Gallica, via Wikimedia Commons.

Identification

Pi II, as catalogued by BnF. SNG France 1, Collection Jean et Marie Delepierre, no. 1469.

BnF specimen catalogue (opens in a new tab) · IRIS type record (opens in a new tab) · Gallica record (opens in a new tab)

Dating

c. 393–295 BCE (broad BnF catalogue range, attributed there to Flament).

Do not silently turn the broad 393–295 BCE record range into the date of Pi II as a whole. The type identification and the chronology should be stored separately.

The obverse offers another detailed view of the helmet and profile eye. Surface marks remain visible: the image has not been “cleaned up” to make an idealized coin.

Heterogeneous is a separate target.

A quadridigité image should not silently stand in for all later Old Style coins. CNG Electronic Auction 594, lot 66, offers a catalogued Heterogeneous Group C specimen: 17.13 g, 24.5 mm; its catalogue gives c. 280/67–229 BCE and cites Nicolet-Pierre and Kroll Group C, 1, “same dies.” That means a die match, not necessarily the same coin.
