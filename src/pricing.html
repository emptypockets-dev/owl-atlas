THE SAME DESIGN IS NOT THE SAME COIN

Start with the object.
Then the number.

A well-centered Athena, a complete helmet crest, an undisturbed surface: details that help us read an owl also shape its appeal to collectors. A wear grade captures only part of that picture. {{CITE:market-ngc}}

For a useful comparison, begin with the same coin family and denomination. Then compare the strike, surfaces and both faces. Keep archaic, classical, Pi-style and New Style results separate.

Why the grades do not form a neat price ladder

The Choice AU sample's median is lower than the AU median because the groups contain different coins. Grade, strike and surface scores do not make two objects identical. NGC grades preservation separately from strike, surfaces and style. {{CITE:market-ngc}}

Two additional Mint State coins were described as brushed or having flan flaws. Including them gives seven MS sales at $1,159–$2,379, with a $2,074 median. The five-coin row excludes those stated problems; it does not certify that the remaining coins are problem-free.

So did the coins on offer.

A closer comparison holds the reported assessment constant: Heritage classical mass issues, NGC Choice XF, strike 5/5 and surface 4/5. Each year still contains different coins.

These examples suggest firmer prices in 2025–2026 than in the earlier sampled years. With only two to five observations per year, they cannot establish a market-wide growth rate. The values are nominal dollars, not adjusted for inflation. Historical Heritage results include 20% buyer premium; the current sample includes 22%. 03 / READ THE WHOLE PRICE

The winning bid
is only the beginning.

The hammer is the winning bid. The buyer premium is the auction house's added fee. Tax and shipping come afterward; the seller's proceeds depend on a separate agreement.

05 / THE EBAY CATCH

“Sold” does not always
reveal the price.

A public eBay page for a classical NGC AU owl displays $2,338.20 beside a sold banner—but also “or Best Offer” and a relisting notice. We cannot treat that as a verified $2,338.20 transaction. A second sold page shows a different Pi-style coin at $999.99; its payment and offer details are also unverified.

Both are retained in the ledger as displayed prices only. Neither enters the auction statistics. Two observations are not enough to establish an eBay price range.

eBay says Product Research supplies three years of actual sale prices, including accepted offers; ordinary completed searches cover 90 days. That account-based dataset was not available for this research snapshot. {{CITE:market-ebay}}

How this snapshot was assembled—and what it cannot tell us

Current Heritage comparisons use two specified consecutive lot ranges, including the coins with listed problems. Historical comparisons are a small, non-random selection of the same reported grade and strike/surface scores at the same auction house. Raw CNG grades are not assumed equivalent to NGC grades. No worldwide average, annual return or price forecast is implied.

Public archive pages were checked on 17 September 2026; some were cached. One August Heritage archive marks a lot sold at $1,442.04 while a cached individual page still shows an earlier live bid. The ledger preserves that conflict and uses the archive result. Several incomplete or conflicting leads were excluded.

No invoice settlement, returns, undisclosed restoration or authenticity was independently verified. Seller net proceeds are unknown. A sell-through rate would also require unsold listings, which this sold-only dataset does not contain.

For a stronger eBay study, actual accepted-offer data should be grouped by type, condition, selling format and period; repeat listings should be deduplicated. Replicas, jewelry, modern bullion and mixed lots need separate treatment. No auction photographs are reproduced here.

This is a dated research snapshot, not a live price feed or an appraisal. The chronological and museum-object dates elsewhere in the atlas remain distinct from auction catalogue descriptions.
