# Owl Atlas: what an Athenian owl costs today

Research date: **17 September 2026**. Prices are USD unless stated otherwise.

**For the common classical silver tetradrachm, this research supports a current auction reference of roughly $1,000–$1,500 for the sampled NGC Choice XF coins and about $2,100–$2,400 for the sampled Mint State coins without listed problems.** Less expensive raw examples with wear or surface issues sold for $450–$1,080 including fees. Selected full-crest coins reached approximately $3,000–$3,400. These are different quality groups, not interchangeable examples of one commodity.

The research contains **64 auction observations and two public eBay sold-listing observations**. It focuses on the classical mass issues, with brief comparisons to other Owl Atlas families. It is a report for review, not a change to the live site.

The [CSV sales ledger](sales.csv) and [JSON data](sales.json) preserve each source, lot number, date basis, reported grade, condition notes, price basis and fee treatment. [Recomputed tables](tables.md) and [machine-readable statistics](summary.json) are generated by [compile.py](compile.py).

## Why two apparently similar owls can differ so much

**Design completeness matters.** A fully visible helmet crest can change the comparison group. In April–June 2026, Heritage reported $3,172 for an AU full-crest owl, $2,928 for another, and $3,416 for a Choice VF star/full-crest coin described as brushed. The latter exceeded every MS result in the 28-lot sample despite its lower wear grade. This demonstrates the limits of grading alone; it does not isolate a universal percentage premium for the crest. [AU, April](https://coins.ha.com/itm/ancients/greek/greek-attica-athens-ca-440-404-bc-ar-tetradrachm-23mm-1718-gm-11h-ngc-au-5-5-4-5-full-crest/a/232617-64024.s), [AU, June](https://coins.ha.com/itm/ancients/greek/greek-attica-athens-ca-440-404-bc-ar-tetradrachm-24mm-1715-gm-10h-ngc-au-5-5-4-5-full-crest/a/61626-23038.s), [Choice VF star, June](https://coins.ha.com/itm/ancients/greek/greek-attica-athens-ca-440-404-bc-ar-tetradrachm-24mm-1718-gm-4h-ngc-choice-vfand-9733-5-5-4-5-full-crest-brushed/a/61626-23039.s).

**Surface condition is independent of wear.** Corrosion, porosity, harsh cleaning, test cuts and other damage can alter desirability even when the design retains considerable detail. A low strike score can also indicate production or centering problems. The dataset retains stated defects rather than mixing them silently into a “typical” value. [NGC on surface quality](https://www.ngccoin.com/news/article/4425/).

**Type must come before price.** “Athenian owl” spans centuries, different artistic traditions and denominations. A classical tetradrachm is not directly comparable to a tiny silver fraction, a Pi-style issue or an archaic rarity. Catalogue dating ranges are retained as the auction houses describe them; they do not replace the site's editorial chronology.

**Provenance and visual appeal need individual comparison.** Record old collections, publication history, style and toning. This dataset does not estimate their separate price effects or imply that a grading holder alone explains the difference between venues.

**My interpretation:** the retrieved examples suggest firmer prices for this particular grade group in 2025–2026 than in the earlier sampled years. They do not establish a market-wide annual growth rate. Two to five observations per year are too few for that, and even identical label grades leave differences in the coins themselves.

The figures are nominal dollars. They are not inflation-adjusted and do not account for ownership costs or seller fees. The included Heritage premiums are generally 20% in the historical records and 22% in the current ones. That fee change alone increases a constant hammer-price result by about 1.7%, which should not be mistaken for rising bidding demand.

### A stronger comparison: one catalog-linked repeat sale

Heritage's July 2025 catalogue identifies its lot 61037 as the coin previously sold in auction 232403, lot 63026. The descriptions agree on 25mm, 17.19g, 8h, and Choice XF 5/5–4/5.

| Appearance | Reported buyer price | Implied hammer, removing 20% fee |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| January 2024, Heritage 232403/63026 | $750 | $625 |
| 2 July 2025, Heritage 232527/61037 | $960 | $800 |

That is **28% more in reported buyer price**, with the same fee rate. The earlier archive gives 18 January as the auction end; the later provenance cites 17 January. Treat this as the January 17–18 sale rather than manufacturing an exact agreement. Identity is linked by the auction catalogue, not independently authenticated through a physical examination. [Earlier result](https://coins.ha.com/c/search/results.zx?archive_state=5327&auction_name=232403&auction_year=2024&dept=1909&layout=list&mode=archive&page=10~6&sb=1&si=2&sold_status=1526), [Later result and explicit provenance](https://coins.ha.com/itm/ancients/greek/greek-attica-athens-ca-440-404-bc-ar-tetradrachm-25mm-1719-gm-8h-ngc-choice-xf-5-5-4-5/a/232527-61037.s).

This was not necessarily a 28% profit. A buyer who paid $750 and later received the $800 hammer would have only $50 before seller commission and other expenses. Actual consignment terms and net proceeds are unknown.

## What eBay contributes—and what remains unverified

I retrieved two public pages with explicit sold banners:

| Listing | Sold date | Described coin | Publicly displayed amount | How to interpret it |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| [267600423498](https://www.ebay.com/itm/267600423498) | 2 July 2026 | Classical tetradrachm, NGC AU | $2,338.20 | “Or Best Offer” and a relisting notice remain visible; actual accepted price is unknown |
| [318418658588](https://www.ebay.com/itm/318418658588) | 1 August 2026 | Pi-style 353–294 BCE, NGC Choice AU | $999.99 | Public sold display; payment/offer price not independently confirmed; $5.99 shipping shown |

The first figure is not a verified $2,338 sale. The second coin belongs to a different family. Neither enters the auction medians, and these two observations cannot establish an eBay price range or a platform premium.

eBay's **Product Research** is the appropriate next source: its official documentation says it provides three years of data and actual sold prices, including accepted Best Offers. Ordinary completed searches cover only the preceding 90 days. Access currently requires the user to complete eBay's verification/sign-in screen; it was not bypassed. [eBay's explanation](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/terapeak?id=4853).

Once access is available, the useful study is a 90-day current sample plus quarterly samples over three years, with these controls:

- Search multiple spellings, including “Athens tetradrachm,” “Athenian owl,” and “Attica tetradrachm”; deduplicate by listing ID and, where possible, specimen/certification number.
- Keep classical mass issues separate from Pi, New Style, regional issues and other denominations.
- Separate auction wins from fixed-price/Best Offer transactions; preserve shipping and currency.
- Exclude declared replicas, jewelry, modern bullion, souvenirs and mixed lots. Mark doubtful attribution/authenticity as unassessed instead of assuming every seller description is correct.
- Inspect both faces and the stated flaws. A price average built only from listing titles is not an adequate comparison.

The result should show sample sizes, medians and spreads by type and condition. A sell-through rate also needs the unsold denominator, which this sold-only dataset does not have.

## Reading a price correctly

**Hammer** is the winning bid. **Buyer price** adds the auction premium. **Delivered cost** also adds applicable tax, shipping and other charges. **Seller proceeds** depend on the consignment agreement and expenses.

CNG explicitly labels the retrieved amounts as excluding its buyer fee; I added the stated 20%. Heritage's reported realized-price convention includes the premium; I did not add it a second time. Its individual lot pages supply the applicable rate. [CNG example](https://cngcoins.com/Lot.aspx?BACK_URL=%2FLots.aspx%3FIS_ADVANCED%3D1%26SEARCH_IN_CONTAINER_TYPE_ID_1%3D1%26SEARCH_IN_CONTAINER_TYPE_ID_3%3D1%26SEARCH_IN_CONTAINER_TYPE_ID_2%3D1%26SEARCH_IN_ITEM_ADD_DATE%3D1%26mm_ITEM_ADD_DATE%3D5%26dd_ITEM_ADD_DATE%3D15%26yyyy_ITEM_ADD_DATE%3D2026%26PAGE_NUM%3D100%26PAGE%3D6&LOT_ID=201456), [Heritage explanation of realized prices](https://coins.ha.com/c/newsletter.zx?frame=no&id=5697), [2026 Heritage lot and 22% rate](https://coins.ha.com/itm/ancients/greek/greek-attica-athens-ca-440-404-bc-ar-tetradrachm-24mm-1715-gm-10h-ngc-au-5-5-4-5-full-crest/a/61626-23038.s).

For example, a $1,200 hammer at 22% becomes $1,464 before tax and shipping. A $1,500 budget before those additional costs implies a maximum hammer of about $1,229.51 at that fee rate; the next permitted bid increment may require a lower bid.

Dealer asking prices, auction estimates, “Make Offer to Owner” invitations and live bids are not completed sale prices. This research does not substitute any of them for a realized result.

## Evidence limits and publication choices

The source pages were retrieved through public web search/open results on the research date. Some are cached. An August Heritage lot page still showed an earlier live bid, while the auction archive marked it sold at $1,442.04; the ledger preserves that conflict and uses the archive result. A direct request to an older Heritage page returned HTTP 403. No account restrictions were bypassed.

The historical sample is deliberately narrow but small and non-random. It overrepresents Heritage's certified market, while the raw comparison comes from CNG. No invoice settlement, returns, authenticity, undisclosed restoration or consignor relationship was independently verified. There is no market-wide index, forecast, or individual appraisal here.

A few promising leads were left out: a 2026 coin citing a 2019 sale whose earlier price could not be recovered; a just-ended September CNG result with conflicting cached page states; and an eBay search result whose complete listing could not be reopened. Future auctions and wrong denominations were also excluded.

If this research becomes site content, the most useful placement would be an optional **“What do owls cost today?”** feature after the historical story. It should carry a visible research date, concrete sold examples, both sides of each coin if image reuse is permitted, separate condition/type groups, and a clear explanation of hammer versus buyer price. It should not assign a dollar value to a museum specimen.

Before publishing that feature, decide the scope, obtain actual eBay offer-price evidence if eBay statistics are desired, review any auction-photo reuse, and choose an update cadence. The present report reproduces no auction photographs and makes no changes to the site's image rights, design or narrative.
